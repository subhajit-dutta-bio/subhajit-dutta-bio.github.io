Telomeres and mitochondria in ageing and in cancer, read through imaging and computation.

Experimental biologist

I study telomeres and mitochondria in ageing and in cancer: how the shelterin protein TRF2 regulates genes away from chromosome ends, and how mitochondria organise local translation at the leading edge of migrating cancer cells, through molecular cell biology, live-cell and super-resolution imaging, and cancer omics.

Data-driven informatician

I build and apply methods for biology at scale: large multi-omics cohorts such as TCGA, DepMap and CPTAC, single-cell and spatial transcriptomics, and high-content image screens. I integrate omics with imaging, and turn the pipelines I write into open tools that others can reuse.

Selected work

Three papers that show the range: a co-first-author mechanism paper, a follow-up on telomerase regulation, and a sole-author quantitative study of cell migration.

bioRxiv · 2026Sole author

Temporal order separates generic persistence from cue-directed commitment in cell migration

Dutta S

Comparing each cell track with the same steps reshuffled shows how much persistence comes from the order of movements, separating how much a cell moves from whether it stays committed to an external cue.

eLife · 2025

Telomeres control human telomerase (TERT) expression through non-telomeric TRF2

Sengupta A, Vinayagamurthy S, Soni D, Deb R, Mukherjee AK, Dutta S, et al.

Telomere length feeds back on telomerase itself: TRF2 bound at the TERT promoter, away from telomeres, recruits PRC2 to repress TERT, and how much TRF2 binds there depends on telomere length.

eLife · 2024Co-first author

Telomere length-sensitive regulation of IL1R1 by the shelterin protein TRF2 modulates immune signalling in the tumour microenvironment

Mukherjee AK*, Dutta S*, Singh A, Sharma S, Roy SS, Sengupta A, et al.

Short telomeres increase TRF2 binding at the IL1R1 promoter, which recruits p300 and switches on IL-1 receptor signalling; in triple-negative breast cancer this tracks with macrophage infiltration.

How my work is different

I work at the bench and at scale, and one question runs through both. It moves from mechanism to image to model, is tested across thousands of tumours, and is checked in independent cohorts before I call it a finding. Public data serves two ways: to test a specific hypothesis at scale, and to let the data suggest new ones.

One question, the whole loop

  1. 1

    Mechanism

    Molecular and cell biology at the bench: CRISPR editing, ChIP, reporter assays, tumour organoids and xenografts.

    TRF2 binding at the IL1R1 promoter, eLife 2024

  2. 2

    Imaging

    Live-cell, super-resolution and FLIM microscopy of the actin cytoskeleton and organelles.

    Mitochondria-associated translational hotspots, thesis 2026

  3. 3

    Models

    Quantitative models of what images and sequencing show: optical flow, nematic order, statistics and machine learning.

    Temporal order in 48,134 cell tracks, bioRxiv 2026

  4. 4

    Scale

    Test the idea across thousands of tumours and cells in TCGA, PCAWG, DepMap, CPTAC and spatial atlases.

    Patient-specific telomere length in TCGA and PCAWG, bioRxiv 2026

  5. 5

    Validation

    Independent cohorts, orthogonal readouts and targeted experiments before calling it a finding.

    Over 90 triple-negative breast tumours, eLife 2024
