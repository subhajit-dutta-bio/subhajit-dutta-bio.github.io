Subhajit Dutta

Cell and molecular biologist working where imaging, computation and cancer meet.

I study how cancer cells organise mitochondria, actin and protein synthesis at the leading edge in order to migrate, and how telomere biology shapes the immune microenvironment of tumours. I pair hands-on molecular cell biology and super-resolution microscopy with variational optical flow, deep learning and large-scale public genomic data.

    Four questions, one cell

    My work runs on two tracks that keep meeting: bench and microscope experiments on how a migrating cancer cell coordinates its organelles, and computational analyses of public genomic, imaging and clinical data at pan-cancer scale.

    Doctoral thesisThe Mitochondria–Actin–Translation Hub

    A physical coupling of OMM-proximal ribosomes, VDAC1-dependent energy supply and actin dynamics at the cell front that allows local translation of protrusion-enabling mRNAs during migration.

    About this project

    Telomeres and immunityHow telomere length talks to the tumour microenvironment

    Non-telomeric TRF2 binding controls IL1R1 and TERT in a telomere-length-sensitive way, linking telomere state to NF-κB signalling and immune infiltration.

    About this project

    Quantitative imagingFrom actin images to nematic fields

    TV-L1 optical flow, orientational order and topological defect analysis applied to live-cell and super-resolution data, with deep-learning pipelines for microscopy.

    About this project

    Pan-cancer computationRegulatory, spatial and immune structure in public data

    Sole-author analyses integrating TCGA, DepMap, CPTAC, ENCODE and spatial transcriptomics to find structure that single studies miss.
